# PART I: THE VOICE MODEL

## 1. How ACP builds a sentence

**He chains. He does not chop.** `[§1, §2.1]`

Long, coordinated, comma-heavy sentences with qualifiers stacked inside them. Measured across six posts he corrected by hand, sentence length *rose* from 9.3 to 10.3 words. When handed a machine draft of short punchy sentences, he merges them.

> `He is a warm and very communicative person, with thoughtful insights and deep thoughts.`
> `A powerhouse that will take a half formed idea and make it shippable in no time (or at least prototypable).`

**Comma splices are voice, not error.** `[§2.2, §4]` Do not repair them.

> `You're three weeks into a sprint, your AI-assisted strategy is sharp.`

**His parenthetical is the plain hyphen.** `[§3]` Four instances: three professional, one fiction. Full count and resolution note in Part V Tier 1.

> `-in my eyes- he has demonstrated a keen attention to detail`
> `she can foresee problems -either in methodology or planning- way before they have an impact`

**His connector is `" : "` with a space either side.** `[§3, §4]` This is how he joins the two halves of a contrast, or introduces a gloss.

> `So the discipline isn't the spec : the discipline is being able to tell whether the output is right or not.`

**Rhythm rule: vary length deliberately.** One long chained sentence, then a short one. Never five medium sentences in a row `[ai-tic-audit B1]`.

**Shape rule: chaining has a ceiling.** Vary sentence *shape*, not only length. After two comma-chained sentences, break with a plain short-shape one. A page of chains is as mechanical as a page of fragments, and it reads flat even when the lengths differ. `[blind-test diff: ACP split the opening chain of a Sonnet draft (`...judgment, and that difference...`) into two sentences and called the original "bland and monotonous"]`

> **Gap in the ear pass.** `ai-tic-audit B1` measures length variation and is blind to shape monotony. A piece can pass B1 with varied word counts while every sentence is the same comma-chain. Check shape by eye; the count will not catch it.

## 2. How ACP builds an argument

**Verdict first, then proof, then the limit.** `[§2.1]`

1. **Open on a categorical judgement**, naming the subject. Not a scene, not a question, not a thesis statement about the state of the world.
   > `Dragos is a highly professional man, that values integrity, sincerity and quality.`
   > `Catalin is heavily driven by a constant search for quality and improvement that permeates every facet of his decisions.`

2. **Prove it with a named artefact.** Five or more instances. He never praises in the abstract; he points at a specific deliverable and says what happened to it.
   > `I will use here the example of the Classified Assignments to demonstrate his impact: Situation/Task... Action... Result:`
   > `Her job on the Projects survey where she took a rough draft of questions and hypotheses and made a coherent survey that was workable is a perfect example of her talent.`

3. **State the limit through `Sometimes`.** Eight instances. Every criticism enters through this hedge, and the hedge is what makes the praise credible.
   > `Sometimes when under stress he can lose sight of the main goal and focus too much on the smaller details`

4. **Admit what you cannot judge.** Two instances. He states the boundary of his own competence rather than bluffing past it.
   > `Difficult for me to judge as i am not knowledgeable on the technical front`

## 3. How ACP opens and closes

**Opening: never announce the structure.** `[§5.4]` Three separate times he deleted the sentence whose only job was to tell the reader what was coming: `Second scenario that breaks designers.`, `Here's a scenario that breaks designers.`, `Here's the line.` Open on the content, or on a spoken hinge.

**Closing: the fronted concessive verdict.** `[§2.1]` This is his signature move and it appears in both English and French, in form-driven and free-form writing. A fronted concessive or appositive phrase, then subject, then verdict. Two-sided, balanced, front-loaded.

> `Sometimes rough and direct but always trying to understand every point of view, Catalin is interesting to talk to`
> `Solid knowledge build through the years but perhaps lacking interest in the newer games, he can bring amazing expertise to any team`
> `Intelligent, driven, commited, Luci is a powerhouse.`
> `Un camarade dévoué, un leader expérimenté et un organisateur efficace, voilà qui est Harley à mes yeux`

**He does not close on a short punch.** The previous version of this file claimed he did. Five diff citations falsify it `[§5.2, §7.2]`.

# PART II: REGISTERS

## 2.1 LinkedIn posts

**Open on the content or a spoken hinge.** Never on a structure announcement `[§5.4]`.

**Spoken hinges he added by hand** `[§1]`. Use sparingly, one per post at most, and read Part III.8 first, because these are the moves most at risk of turning into costume.
> `Huh..` · `Well then, i do believe that` · `But hear this..` · `Makes sense isn't it ?` · `Crazy idea, i know, but`

**Hedge your own claims.** `[§1, §2.5]` He adds qualifiers to his own assertions and deflates his own credentials.
> `Humans still make the decision (or should).`
> `It's live, works and still i am amazed i could make it.`

**Self-deprecating aside carries the point.** `[§2.5]` His longest comments are built around a joke at his own expense, not around an insight.
> `I'm still actively trying to crash a board by loading as many high-res images and PDFs as humanly possible. It barely loads... but it does load. That's a win in my book 🙂`

**Emoji are normal here.** 🙂 😉 👏 `[§2.5]`

**Closers.** For comments, the generosity closer: end by handing something to the other person `[§2.5]`.
> `Can try to find that skill again and share it to you if you want 🙂`

**Every sentence advances the argument.** Never restate a point in a second costume. If the piece runs short of substance, that is a signal the context is thin, not a licence to pad. **Ask ACP for the anecdote or the example in-session.** `[blind-test: ACP scored a padded post 6/10, "it kinda rambled on and on"]`

**Never:** hashtag blocks `[§5.7, removed 6/6]` · a question-to-audience closer `[§7.2, zero corpus instances]` · a quotable one-liner punchline `[§5.3]`.

## 2.2 Cover letters and self-advocacy

> **This section is deliberately unfamiliar to ACP. That is intended, and certified by the tower.**
>
> Its structure comes from how he writes about *other people* `[§2.1]`, not from his own cover letters `[§2.7.2]`. His three letters are one maintained template, 82% verbatim from 2020 to 2025, and three of their moves are on the ban lists derived from his own editing hand `[§2.7.4]`. Drafts written to this section will not feel like his letters. Read the unfamiliarity as the intended change, not as drift.

**The principle: write about ACP the way ACP writes about the people he works with.** `[§2.7.4, certified]`

The contrast that produced this rule: in his referrals he names a specific artefact as proof five or more times, admits his own limits twice, and closes on a two-sided verdict four or more times. In his cover letters he does none of those, hedges the central claim (`I think I can join your team`, 3/3), and defers his evidence rather than presenting it (`I can refer on demand to the various positive feedback that has been given to me`, 3/3).

### Structure

1. **Categorical opener naming the match.** State what he is and why it fits this role. No abstract world-state, no `The digital world today is a complicated one` `[§2.7.4, §5.4]`. No flattery opener `[ai-tic-audit B2]`.
2. **Why them.** One short researched paragraph: what the company is actually doing, why that matters, and what they get from him joining. Specific and verifiable. No flattery adjectives, no puffery `[ai-tic-audit A7]`. Corpus basis: the researched-admiration opening of the 2019 letter, the only fresh composition of the three, modernized. `[blind-test: ACP on the blind letter, "it needs to show that you care for the business and you know it... and how integrating the team will benefit them"]`
3. **One named artefact as proof.** Situation, what he did, what happened. Specific, singular, verifiable. This replaces the tool list and the methodology chain `[§2.1, §2.7.4]`.
4. **The limit-bearing close. Mandatory.** `[tower-ruled]` The honest limit and the fronted concessive verdict are **one move, not two**. The limit *is* the concessive half of the verdict:
   > `[limit] but [strength], [verdict]`

   Never state the limit as a standalone sentence and then restate it inside the close. That reads as a stutter. No justification clause, no editorializing tail `[Part III.6]`.

   **Corpus:** every referral closer embeds the limit inside the verdict, and no referral states it twice `[§2.1]`.
   > `Sometimes rough and direct but always trying to understand every point of view, Catalin is interesting to talk to`

   **Worked example** `[blind-test round 2: executed as two separate steps, the letter stated the limit back-to-back in two forms. ACP flagged the sequence as redundant, scored 7/10]`
   > **Two steps, wrong:** `i tend to take risks before checking guidelines for the test.` followed by `Quick to move and not always patient with the guidelines first, I get there anyway by testing hard and fixing fast.`
   > **One move, right:** `I tend to take risks before checking guidelines for the test, but I get there by testing hard and fixing fast.`

   **When ACP supplies the limit line himself, his words become the concessive half verbatim.** Exempt from the tricolon rescope in Part III.5.

   > **Scope of "ships as given"** `[tower-ruled, round 2]`. It governs ACP's **words**; Part V Tier 2 governs **orthography**. His phrasing is untouchable. Capitalization and spelling in Swiss-bound formal drafts follow the Tier 2 standardize-and-note procedure. The two rules share the sentence without conflict.
5. **Logistics.** Location, availability, languages, and **citizenship, which is mandatory**. For Swiss-bound letters the Swiss citizenship is never omitted `[tower catch: the blind letter shipped without it]`. Plain and short.

### Salutation and sign-off `[§7.6, tower-ruled]`

**Use a named recipient where one is known.** Otherwise `Dear [Company] Team`. Do not use `Dear Sir, Madam,` even though it appears in 3/3 letters, modernized by ruling.

**Sign off plainly.** `Looking forward to hearing from you.` or equivalent.

**Recorded as history, not as rules:** the calqued sign-off `With pleasure of your contact, receive Sir, Madam, my best regards.` (3/3 letters, calques `Dans l'attente de votre contact, recevez, Madame, Monsieur, mes meilleures salutations`) and `annexe : curriculum vitae` (3/3). These are genuinely his and they are genuinely awkward in English. They are logged so the record is complete. They are not to be reproduced.

### Banned in this register `[§2.7.4]`

| Move | Why |
|---|---|
| Abstract world-state opener | Same family as the scaffolding cold opens he deleted 3× `[§5.4]` |
| Unevidenced character claim (`As a hard working and responsible person`) | Claims what an artefact should demonstrate |
| Deferred proof (`I can refer on demand to...`) | Present the evidence, do not point at it |
| Tool and software lists | Assumed at senior level; the artefact carries more |
| `I think I can join your team` | Hedges the one claim the letter exists to make |
| Semicolon or comma triplets | Cut to pairs `[§5.6, 3 diff citations]` |

## 2.4 Casual (EN and FR)

**Casual FR** `[§2.3, N=3, observations, not rules]`
Opener `Yop [name] !` · address `bro` / `mec` · sign-off `Pop` / `Pops` · `ahah` for laughter · `:)` as softener · space before `!` · English dropped mid-sentence (`Check whatsapp aussi`).

**Formal FR** `[§2.4, N=2, but both instances share one structure]`
1. Thanks first: `Je vous remercie pour votre mail` / `Je vous remercie de revenir vers moi`
2. Bad news through `Malheureusement`
3. Counter-offer through `Cependant` or `Éventuellement`
4. Availability formula, then `Cordialement,`

**Correction to the availability formula** `[tower]`: this is a **pattern with a fixed stem, not a verbatim line.** Both instances vary the tail:
> `Je vous remercie et reste à votre disposition pour toute question.`
> `Je reste à votre disposition pour toutes informations.`

Use the stem `reste à votre disposition pour...` and let the tail fit the situation. Do not treat either variant as canonical.

He also states the *reason* for a request plainly rather than dressing it up:
> `Payer 300.- en une fois ferait disparaître 1/3 de mon salaire directement.`

**Casual EN** `[§2.6]`
`bro` / `brother` address, corroborated outside chat. Elongation for emphasis (`a loooong time ago`, `c'est coooooool`), two instances `[§3]`.

**Do not export from chat:** `buddy`, `Chadster`, `errthang`, `ah ah`, `final final`. These have no corroboration outside his conversations with Claude `[§2.6]`.

# PART III: ANTI-TIC

Aligned with `ai-tic-audit`. That skill counts and flags; this section prevents.

## 3.1 The absolutes

**1. Never write an em dash.** `[§5.1, 9 instances in the drafts, 9 killed, 6/6 pairs, 100%]`

The single strongest finding in the corpus, and independently the top-cited tell in `ai-tic-audit A1`. His replacements, taken from his own edits: a period, a comma, `but`, or `" : "`.

**2. Never leave assistant boilerplate.** `[ai-tic-audit, absolute]`

## 3.2 The split ban

**Contrast is welcome. It lives inside one sentence. It never crosses a full stop.** `[§5.2, §6, tower-adopted as written]`

He does not delete the contrast. **He deletes the period between its halves.** Five citations, all the same operation:

> `That's not vibe coding. That's directed building...` → `That's not vibe coding but directed building...`
> `internally coherent. That's not the same as right.` → `internally coherent, but that's not the same as it being right.`
> `a skeleton. Real users give you the flesh.` → `a skeleton, but real users give you the flesh.`
> `isn't the spec. The discipline is...` → `isn't the spec : the discipline is...`
> `...do the work. Design for humans...` → `...do the work, design for humans...`

**Join with:** `but` · `,` · `" : "`

**A model told simply "no negative parallelism" will strip contrast out of his writing and produce something flat.** The ban is on the split, never on the contrast.

## 3.3 The concessive carve-out

**This protects a real limb of the voice. Getting it wrong amputates his best move.** `[§6, tower-adopted as written]`

**ACP's concessive pair: PRESERVE.** Two claims, **both asserted as true**, joined inside one sentence by `but` or `though`. The second qualifies the first; it does not cancel it.
> `Sometimes rough and direct but always trying to understand every point of view`
> `His animations were simple but precise with a correct timing`
> `Very solid and dependable at all times (...) but when pushed too much, can be snappy`

**Machine negative parallelism: BAN.** A negated claim **the writer does not hold**, split off by a period, existing only to be knocked down.
> `It sounds humane. It is not.`
> `The fix isn't to stop using AI. The fix is to keep building craft...`
> `It's not a parsing problem. It's a human one.`

**Two tests, in order:**
1. **Is the first half something he actually believes?** Concessive: yes, both halves are his real assessment. Parallelism: no, the first half exists to be demolished.
2. **What joins them?** Concessive: a comma or `but`, inside one sentence. Parallelism: a period.

## 3.4 The aphorism cap

**One plain-worded epigram per piece, maximum. Every other quotable closer gets rewritten as talking.** `[§5.3, tower-adopted as written]`

Eight epigram closers appeared across the six drafts. One survived his edit.

Dissolved: `Direction without Craft is sophistication theatre.` → `You've got a Direction, but with no Craft under it, it's expensive theatre.`
Dissolved: `Speed in the wrong direction is the most expensive kind of speed.` → absorbed into a three-clause chained sentence.
Survived: `Direction without Contact is confidence without ground truth.`

The survivor uses plain words and anchors a recurring series. The one that died in the identical construction carried the ornate abstract noun. **The rule is not "never write a memorable line." It is "at most one, and it must use plain words."**

## 3.5 Vocabulary ban list

Imported from `ai-tic-audit A4`, core tier. Never write:

> delve · tapestry · leverage · seamless · underscore · pivotal · meticulously · embark · harness · unlock (metaphorical) · elevate (metaphorical) · curate (non-museum) · boasts · game-changer · showcase · testament

Low-weight tier (`navigate`, `robust`, `align`, `enhance`, `foster`, `comprehensive`, `crucial`, `utilize`, `however`, `moreover`, `furthermore`) is only a problem when three or more cluster in a short span. A lone `however` is ordinary prose, not a tell `[ai-tic-audit C]`.

Also cut, from his own hand `[§5.8]`: corporate-register verbs where a plain one exists. He changed `articulate the vision` to `pitch the whole vision`.

**Additional bans traceable to the diffs:**
- **Tricolons, in argumentative prose only.** Three-item lists inside an argument get cut to two, three citations `[§5.6]`.
  **Rescoped `[tower-certified, corpus beats file]`.** The fronted-verdict triplet in professional register is his hand and is preserved:
  > `Intelligent, driven, commited, Luci is a powerhouse.`
  > `Un camarade dévoué, un leader expérimenté et un organisateur efficace, voilà qui est Harley à mes yeux`
- **Hashtag blocks.** Removed 6/6 `[§5.7]`.

## 3.6 No meta-commentary on the writing

**Say the thing. Never announce the saying.** `[blind-test: ACP cut both instances by hand, "no human would ever write like that"]`

Ban every sentence that narrates its own directness or frames the act of writing:
> `that's worth naming plainly rather than glossing over` · `to be blunt` · `I'll say it straight` · `I would rather say that directly than let the title imply otherwise`

This overlaps the honest-limit step in Part II.2. The limit is stated flat and the sentence ends. The moment a draft explains *why* it is being honest, it has stopped being honest and started performing.

## 3.7 No verbatim phrase echo

**A distinctive phrase appears once per piece. Never twice.** `[blind-test]`

The blind letter used `the exact discipline...calls for` and `exactly the discipline this role is asking for` inside 167 words. The blind feedback opened and closed on `highly creative`. Ordinary connectives are exempt; this covers any phrase a reader would notice returning.

Check by scanning for repeated three-word-or-longer strings before delivering.

**Scope: shipped pieces only.** A post, a letter, a feedback, an email. Reference documents legitimately repeat defined terms, so this rule does not apply to skill files or specs, where restating a term is how a spec works.

## 3.8 The over-correction trap

`ai-tic-audit A11` names the 2026 tell: prose visibly straining not to read as AI. It flags a forced lowercase `i`, a bolted-on casual opener, and conspicuous em-dash avoidance.

**This is a real risk for this file specifically, so the rule is strict:**

**Never manufacture a fingerprint.** Never inject a lowercase `i`, never fake a typo, never staple on a casual hinge. Part V is a set of *preservation* rules. If a marker appears naturally, it stays. It is never added to make a draft sound more like him.

**One live conflict, handled rather than ignored.** `ai-tic-audit A1` says not to swap a colon in for a deleted em dash, because readers now flag that as a dodge. This file says `" : "` is his native connector. Both are right. The distinction is where it sits:

- **Native use, keep it.** joining the two halves of a contrast, or introducing a gloss or a list. He does this in cover letters written six years before the tic-audit existed `[§2.7.3, 15 instances corpus-wide]`.
- **Dodge, cut it.** a colon standing in the exact spot where an em dash was just deleted. That is the A11 contortion. Use a comma or a period instead.

**Do not let the not-AI moves become uniform.** One spoken hinge per LinkedIn post, not one per paragraph.

# PART IV: THE HONESTY RULE

**Never invent a number, a timeline, or a vivid specific. Never include internal or confidential detail.** `[§5.5, 5 citations across 3 pairs]`

He strips both, by hand, every time:

> `85% of your user research` → `your user research` *(fabricated statistic)*
> `Master, Pathfinder, Easygoer, Teamplayer, Newcomer` → deleted *(internal persona names)*
> `one offhand comment a player made in a transcript, three hours into reading` → `comments and off hand remarks that kept popping up consistently` *(invented scene and timeline)*
> `I've watched players make purchase decisions in TD2 for five years` → paragraph deleted *(tenure claim)*
> `I've watched smart teams ship internally coherent strategies straight into a wall` → replaced with a hedged first-person account

**Operative rule:** if a number, date, metric, or scene is not supplied by ACP in the session or verifiable in Part VII, it does not go in the draft. Write the honest plural version instead. This is stated as a rule; no speculation on motive.

**Every source, link, and statistic must be verified or absent** `[ai-tic-audit A10]`.

### The answered self-question `[blind-test + corpus]`

He asks himself a question, often French-spaced, and answers it on the spot.
> `Makes sense isn't it ? Well...`
> `But for shipping ? You need to talk to your customers.`
> `Hosting dinner for four. My solution? An incredible roast chicken recipe.`
> `Would i still make it today ? Yes, but keeping my eyes open this time.` *(blind-test rework)*

**Boundary against the banned question-to-audience closer `[§7.2]`:** he answers his own question. He never fishes for replies. A question left hanging at the end of a piece is the banned move; a question answered in the next clause is this one.

### The `i do think` hedge `[diff 3.4 + blind-test]`

> `Well then, i do believe that half of this is correct` *(diff 3.4)*
> `i do think a Senior UX/UI Designer role calls for that pression.` *(blind-test rework)*

**Boundary against the `I think I can join your team` ban `[Part II.2]`:** that ban targets hedging the letter's *central ask*. This hedge softens an *opinion*. Soften what he believes; never soften what he is asking for.

# EXAMPLES

Real edits ACP made by hand to AI drafts. The annotation is why.

### 1. The split ban `[§5.2]`

> **Draft:** `That's not vibe coding. That's directed building with a fluent collaborator.`
> **ACP:** `That's not vibe coding but directed building with a fluent collaborator.`

He kept the negation and removed the period. This is the single clearest illustration of Part III.2: the contrast was never the problem, the full stop was.

### 2. The honesty rule `[§5.5]`

> **Draft:** `The five-persona framework I use — Master, Pathfinder, Easygoer, Teamplayer, Newcomer — only exists because we sat with raw survey data for weeks. Pathfinders and Teamplayers look identical on the surface. Same behaviors. Opposite motivations.`
> **ACP:** `The five-persona framework I use only exists because we sat with raw survey data and did interviews for weeks. Two personas looked identical on the surface with the same behaviors. Turned out they had opposite motivations.`

Four things at once. Internal persona names stripped. Em dashes gone. Two sentence fragments absorbed into full sentences. And `and did interviews` added, because that is what actually happened.

### 3. The aphorism cap `[§5.3]`

> **Draft:** `Direction without Craft is sophistication theatre. You're not a designer using AI. You're an editor who can't read.`
> **ACP:** `You've got a Direction, but with no Craft under it, it's expensive theatre. You've become an editor who can't read.`

The epigram dissolved into a running sentence joined by `but`. The negated half of the parallelism deleted outright, leaving only the claim he actually makes. `sophistication` became `expensive`.

### 4. Invented specifics to hedged first person `[§5.5]`

> **Draft:** `I've watched smart teams ship internally coherent strategies straight into a wall. The signal that something is off rarely comes from the strategy doc. It comes from one offhand thing a user says in a hallway — and you only hear it if you're still in the hallway.`
> **ACP:** `I've watched it from the inside, you know, a plan the whole org had blessed, the plan that sails suspiciously easily through a leadership review. But it lacked the thing that should have stopped it, one sentence in a research session nobody senior bothered to attend. Because, the signal that something's off rarely shows up in the strategy doc. It shows up in one offhand thing a user says in a hallway.`

The confident generic claim became a specific hedged account. `you know,` and `Because,` added as verbal filler. The em dash and its clever callback both cut. Note the direction of travel: longer, more chained, less quotable.
